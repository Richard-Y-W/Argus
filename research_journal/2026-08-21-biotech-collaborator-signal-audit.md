# Biotech collaborator-signal audit — 2026-08-21

Richard proposed extending the clinical-evidence project beyond trial text to collaborator behavior: purchases of syringes, insurance changes, and other operational preparation that might precede abrupt repricing.

The idea improved after separating activity from belief. An outside party's costly, asset-specific, difficult-to-reverse commitment may aggregate technical diligence. Generic ancillary procurement is weaker: the US COVID distribution plan explicitly contemplated stockpiling supplies across several candidates before regulatory decisions. Insurance coverage is generally too stale and nonspecific for the lead test.

The engineering slice now represents public signals with separate action and publication dates, asset specificity, and an availability-precision label. Current ClinicalTrials.gov or USAspending responses are marked `retrieval_only`; they cannot be backdated into a historical information set.

No outcomes or returns were analyzed. Moderna is retained only as an outcome-known schema example. The next admissible work is a balanced, return-blind 30-event ledger that measures source availability and dual-coder reliability before any hypothesis registration.

## Adversarial verdict

**Feasibility:** supported with limits. The public source families can populate the proposed ledger.  
**Early-signal claim:** inconclusive. The same behavior is consistent with efficacy belief, manufacture-at-risk, government risk sharing, or expected demand.  
**Tradability:** not assessed. A public collaborator announcement may itself cause repricing, and no return or execution data have been inspected.

Strongest objection: external commitments are endogenous financial options, not clean forecasts. A partner can rationally reserve capacity for a low-probability, high-payoff product—especially when the sponsor or government absorbs the downside. The pilot must test incremental outcome information beyond base rates and these financing variables.
