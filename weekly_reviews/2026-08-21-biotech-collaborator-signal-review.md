# Adversarial review: biotech collaborator signals

**Reviewed stage:** exploration and source-feasibility scaffold  
**Verdict:** INCONCLUSIVE for prediction; SUPPORTED WITH LIMITS for ledger feasibility  
**Promotion:** prohibited pending a return-blind historical pilot

## Claim under review

Publicly disclosed preparation by collaborators may provide an early signal of pivotal clinical success before the sponsor's stock reprices.

## Evidence audit

- **Statistical:** No empirical outcome sample exists. Power, prevalence, calibration, and incremental log loss are unknown. A 30-event pilot is for feasibility and coder reliability, not a final effect estimate.
- **Econometric:** Partner selection, funding, sponsor quality, disease area, expected market size, and scheduled readouts jointly affect commitments and outcomes. A raw success-rate comparison would not identify independent information.
- **Economic:** Capacity reservation can be a rational option even at low success probability. Government guarantees can make a nominally costly signal cheap to the counterparty. Supply preparation may reveal demand rather than efficacy.
- **Engineering:** Action dates are not publication timestamps. Current ClinicalTrials.gov and USAspending records can contain future information. The scaffold now blocks retrieval-only records from qualifying historically.
- **Communication:** “Early signal” currently means a candidate predictor of clinical outcome, not alpha, causality, private information, or net profitability.

## Signal-level decisions

| Proposed observable | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-specific third-party manufacturing expansion | Retain | Costly and plausibly diligence-bearing, but flag reimbursement and risk sharing |
| Milestone-bearing licensing/co-development | Retain | External capital at risk; terms and timing must be public |
| Asset-specific government award | Retain with control | Government objectives and downside absorption are major confounders |
| Trial collaborator listing | Retain as weak feature | Often administrative and not intrinsically costly |
| Generic syringe/vial procurement | Negative control | Multi-candidate preparedness is not asset-specific |
| Insurance limit change | Deprioritize | Stale annual disclosure and weak connection to one clinical asset |
| Supplier stock reaction | Separate study | Measures exposure at readout, not pre-readout prediction |

## Required next evidence

Build 30 outcome-balanced Phase III events without inspecting returns. Prespecify source families and the 30–365-day window, include failed/delisted sponsors, dual-code asset specificity and costly commitment, and require at least 80% bounded public timestamps, 90% asset-mapping agreement, and 10 qualifying events. Only after those gates pass may Argus register an outcome-prediction experiment; returns remain a later stage.

## Red-team conclusion

The strongest surviving form of Richard's idea is narrower than procurement surveillance: an independently disclosed, costly, asset-specific commitment may contain aggregated outside diligence. Nothing collected so far shows that it predicts success or prices. Generic syringe purchases would have produced a persuasive but invalid COVID story, so they are deliberately retained as a control rather than promoted as evidence.
