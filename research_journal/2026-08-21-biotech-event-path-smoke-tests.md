# Biotech event-path smoke tests — 2026-08-21

Richard added a symmetric trading question to the eight-candidate program: if catching an announcement rise is difficult, can a systematic short capture a subsequent fall? Argus froze the exact rule before retrieving prices and preserved the negative result.

Among 16 positive Hwang events, 10 had positive SPY-adjusted returns through close +1. Shorting from close +1 to close +20 lost 1.15% gross on average and 1.65% after a fixed 50 bp cost; 40% were positive and the t interval was wide. The rule failed rather than merely lacking significance because its frozen direction, cost-adjusted mean, and breadth criteria all failed.

Two related premise checks were also run. Positive and negative events had almost identical mean SPY-adjusted returns from -120 to -3 (+9.27% and +8.52%), so this seed does not reproduce a strong outcome-separated run-up. Eight large-firm failures returned +2.37% XBI-adjusted from +2 to +100 on average, but the median was -0.62%, only half were positive, and one +43.86% BIIB observation dominated the mean. This is a wrong-universe placebo, not a test of the proposed small-biotech reversal.

The eight candidate families were then audited against available data. Scientific-quality drift, small-biotech reversal, and public run-up decomposition have partial price-path checks; broad collaborator presence was previously rejected; competitor mapping, options, historical operational updates, and financing residuals remain behind explicit data gates. No missing construct was replaced with a convenient but invalid proxy.

## Decision

Do not tune spike cutoffs or exits on the consumed Hwang seed. Build the survivorship-safe small-biotech Phase III failure universe first. In parallel, build the return-blind evidence-quality ledger. The positive-spike short can be one disclosed interaction in a development/untouched split after those gates pass.
