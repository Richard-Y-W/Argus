# Factor dependence and effective breadth

*Reviewed 2026-07-26. AI-led synthesis for EXP-022–024.*

Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen (2023, *Journal of Finance*, DOI 10.1111/jofi.13249) argue that the factor zoo is not a collection of independent discoveries. Their 153 factors collapse into 13 economically and statistically related themes, and their joint empirical-Bayes model uses a block correlation structure because treating related factors independently misstates information. This is directly relevant to Argus: EXP-020's 141 factor contrasts and its conventional cross-factor t-statistic do not establish 141 independent pieces of evidence.

Harvey, Liu, and Zhu (2016, *Review of Financial Studies*, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhu105) make the broader point that dependence and multiple testing change the evidentiary threshold in a factor zoo. Their question concerns discovery tests, while Argus's question concerns dependence among already-published factor-level decay contrasts; the methods are not interchangeable. The transferable principle is that the nominal count is not the information count.

The gap is narrow but consequential. Existing Argus audits equal-weight factors and delete them one at a time, yet neither measures effective breadth nor asks whether a correlated family carries the result. EXP-022–024 therefore estimate pre-publication dependence, conduct family-level exact inference, and delete whole empirical families. These are identification audits of an existing return association, not new evidence of publication-induced trading.
