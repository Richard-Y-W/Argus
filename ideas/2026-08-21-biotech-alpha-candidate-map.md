# Biotech alpha candidate map after the collaborator-proxy rejection

**Stage:** exploration and test prioritization  
**Attribution:** Collaborative  
**Boundary:** no new return result; none of these candidates is an alpha claim

## What the first failure changes

The Hwang current-record probe rejected the broad idea that a collaborator listing is itself informative. The next candidates must use changes, surprises, economic exposure, and exact public timestamps. A level such as “has a partner” is likely stale and endogenous.

## Candidate 1 — scientific-quality-conditioned post-readout drift

After a company releases a positive result, does the market initially price the headline but underweight evidence quality?

Freeze a return-blind evidence rubric using effect size, confidence interval, endpoint hierarchy, clinical meaningfulness, multiplicity adjustment, subgroup consistency, attrition, safety, comparator quality, and regulatory precedent. Test CAR[+2,+20] and CAR[+2,+60], conditional on outcome sign, immediate CAR[0,+1], market capitalization, lead-asset exposure, liquidity, and simultaneous news.

- Mechanism: generalist investors process “endpoint met” faster than scientific robustness.
- Falsifier: evidence quality has no incremental out-of-sample relation with drift, or the effect disappears after exact timestamps and microcap exclusions.
- Data feasibility: relatively high; the released evidence and timestamp are directly observable.
- Practical threat: the drift may be too small after spread, borrow, halts, and overnight gaps.

## Candidate 2 — small-biotech failure reversal

Replicate the 2025 claim that small firms overreact to Phase III failures. The frozen strategy buys eligible failed-readout firms after the announcement window and measures sector-adjusted returns through day +100, with delistings, dilution, bankruptcy, borrow constraints, and cash burn included.

- Mechanism: forced selling and lottery-belief collapse push diversified-pipeline or cash-rich firms below post-failure value.
- Alternative: the apparent reversal is omitted delistings, survivor selection, overlapping financing announcements, or ordinary microcap risk.
- Falsifier: the published direction fails on a reconstructed sample, vanishes value-weighted, or is consumed by execution and financing losses.
- Novelty: low as a hypothesis, high value as an adversarial replication because the reported 40–50% strategy return is unusually large.

## Candidate 3 — commitment surprise and counterparty skin in the game

Replace collaborator presence with the change in external exposure:

`commitment surprise = new asset-specific external capital or capacity / sponsor market value`

Separate non-refundable upfront cash, milestone-bearing licenses, dedicated manufacturing investment, sponsor-reimbursed capacity, and government-guaranteed procurement. First test clinical outcomes; returns enter only after that gate.

- Mechanism: a sophisticated counterparty reveals costly technical diligence.
- Alternative: real-option value, expected market size, sponsor reimbursement, or government risk sharing.
- Falsifier: no outcome-information gain beyond phase, disease, sponsor size, financing, and scheduled-readout controls.

## Candidate 4 — mechanism-matched competitor read-through

When one asset reports, identify publicly traded competitors with the same target, modality, indication, and development stage. Test whether the initial competitor reaction underweights how transferable the scientific news is.

- Positive trial: may validate the biological target but increase commercial competition.
- Negative trial: may invalidate the target or remove a competitor.
- The sign must be predicted from a frozen biological/competitive decision tree, not inferred from the observed return.
- Main threat: target validation and competitive effects oppose one another, making hand-coded hindsight especially dangerous.

## Candidate 5 — public run-up decomposition

Earlier research reported substantially different pre-announcement returns for successful and unsuccessful Phase III products. Test whether point-in-time public variables explain that divergence: external commitments, registry amendments, readout-timing revisions, Form 4 transactions, financing, short interest, and prior related science.

This is primarily a mechanism test. Any unexplained run-up could reflect private-information leakage and is not automatically a public trading signal.

## Candidate 6 — options-implied versus scientific probability

Translate pre-event options into an implied move/distribution and compare it with a frozen clinical success and valuation model. Test calibrated expected value rather than raw direction.

- Strength: directly measures market uncertainty.
- Weakness: historical option quotes, borrow, volatility surface quality, and event timing are expensive and difficult; a wrong valuation model can masquerade as probability alpha.

## Candidate 7 — operational timing for volatility, not direction

Use enrollment changes, site expansion, primary-completion revisions, data-lock language, and conference scheduling to forecast when a readout becomes imminent. The target is event timing or realized volatility, not whether the drug works.

This may be more identifiable than efficacy prediction because these signals reflect operational progress. It still requires historical registry versions and exact update availability.

## Candidate 8 — financing and insider-action residuals

Test whether point-in-time ATM usage, offerings, debt, insider purchases/sales, and 10b5-1 disclosures predict outcomes or post-event magnitude after controlling for cash runway and planned financing. SEC filings provide exact public timestamps; Form 4 transactions generally arrive within two business days.

The strongest alternative is mechanical financing need: raising cash before a binary event may be prudent risk management rather than negative private information.

## Candidate 9 — positive-spike reversal

Short a positive clinical event only after a measured initial abnormal rise. The first frozen large-company smoke test entered at close +1 and exited at close +20. It failed: 10 eligible events produced -1.15% mean gross short abnormal return, -1.65% after a fixed 50 bp cost, and 40% positive outcomes. Do not tune it on the consumed sample. A future small-biotech interaction remains logically distinct and requires borrow, halt, delisting, and lead-asset exposure data.

## Internal debate

- **Optimist:** scientific complexity, attention diffusion, and outside diligence are exactly where a specialized evidence model could add value.
- **Skeptic:** most large jumps resolve genuinely blinded uncertainty; public breadcrumbs may predict timing and demand but not efficacy.
- **Statistician:** eight feature families create serious multiple-testing exposure. Promote at most one primary return endpoint and preserve a later untouched period.
- **Economist:** partner capital reveals option value and market size as well as belief; financing and government guarantees change the meaning of “skin in the game.”
- **Portfolio manager:** overnight gaps, halts, microcap spreads, dilution, borrow recalls, and delistings can destroy an attractive gross backtest.
- **ML researcher:** do not train a flexible text or graph model until a dual-coded gold ledger establishes stable labels and source timestamps.

## Priority decision

Run two ordered tracks, not eight parallel backtests:

1. **Replication track:** failure reversal, because the existing 40–50% claim is direct, testable, and suspiciously large.
2. **Original track:** scientific-quality drift, because it directly tests whether difficult biomedical evidence is incorporated slowly and has better point-in-time data than pre-readout commitments.

Commitment surprise continues as the original pre-readout branch, but it remains behind the historical-ledger gate. All other candidates stay queued until one of the two primary tracks is resolved.
