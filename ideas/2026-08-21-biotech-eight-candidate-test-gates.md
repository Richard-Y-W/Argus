## Candidate 9 — short after a positive announcement spike

The rule was frozen before prices were retrieved: positive paper-coded event; positive SPY-adjusted reaction through close +1; short at close +1; cover at close +20; charge 50 bp round trip. Ten of 16 positive events were eligible. Mean short abnormal return was -1.15% gross and -1.65% after cost, the median was -0.50%, and only 40% were positive. The frozen rule failed.

This result rejects only this exact large-company rule. It does not resolve whether extreme spikes in small, lead-asset-dependent biotechnology firms reverse, because the Hwang seed consists of large pharmaceutical and biotechnology firms and lacks borrow, halt, delisting, and announcement-time treatment.

## Decision

Do not optimize the failed short rule's threshold or holding period on these events. Continue with the two ordered primary tracks already selected:

1. reconstruct and adversarially replicate the small-biotech failure-reversal study; and
2. create a return-blind scientific-quality ledger for positive-readout drift.

The short-after-spike variant may return only as a frozen interaction inside the future small-biotech ledger, with the spike threshold selected on a development period and evaluated once on untouched events.

## Reproducibility and limitations

The calculations use adjusted closes from Yahoo's mutable public chart endpoint, SPY or XBI subtraction, and a tiny published event seed. They omit factor models, borrow fees and availability, bid-ask spreads beyond the fixed cost, intraday announcement times, halts, delistings, simultaneous news, dependence from repeated firms, and multiple-event overlap. They are sandbox diagnostics, not evidence of alpha or advice to trade.
