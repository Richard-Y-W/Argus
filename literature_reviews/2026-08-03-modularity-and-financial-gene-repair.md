# Modularity and financial gene repair

Kashtan and Alon (2005) show in computational biology that modularly varying goals can select modular networks. Kashtan et al. (2007) further show that environments sharing subproblems can accelerate adaptation. Clune, Mouret, and Lipson (2013) demonstrate that connection-cost pressure can produce networks that are both more modular and more evolvable. These papers support a conditional mechanism: local repair should help only when the problem really decomposes and some subproblems remain stable.

They do not establish a financial edge. Portfolio weights are not DNA, realized returns are not organismal fitness, and industry portfolios are overlapping economic systems. Genetic and evolutionary algorithms are already common optimization tools in finance; the narrower question is whether an independently diagnosed module restriction adds value beyond equal-budget global and unrestricted local search.

EXP-025–027 already rejected three broad controller designs on Argus's synthetic generator. EXP-028 therefore changes both the data family and the estimand. It tests modular credit assignment on official historical industry returns, with a frozen archive and a later-vintage check.

## Gap and alternatives

The proposed contribution is not “genetic algorithms in finance.” It is the smaller mechanism claim that limiting mutation to modules with observed distributional damage can reduce destructive search when other modules remain useful. Alternatives are that the detector is noisy, financial modules are too coupled, five genes are too few, or ordinary shrinkage/minimum variance dominates search entirely.
