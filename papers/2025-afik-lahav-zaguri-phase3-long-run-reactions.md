# Afik, Lahav, and Zaguri (2025): long-term Phase III announcement reactions

**Citation:** Zvika Afik, Yaron Lahav, and Bat-El Zaguri, “Long-term market reactions to FDA Phase III clinical trials announcements,” *Finance Research Letters* 86, 108139. DOI: `10.1016/j.frl.2025.108139`.

## Claim

The paper reports 92 Phase III success/failure events for US-listed pharmaceutical companies from 2011–2019. It claims that failure announcements underreact for large firms but overreact for small firms, with a post-failure strategy returning roughly 40–50% through 100 days.

## Accessible methodology

The authors identify events from BioSpace, exclude multiple-drug observations for the same company by retaining the earliest event, measure size using equity market value, and examine long cumulative average abnormal-return windows. The accessible article record does not expose the event table, exact size breakpoint, security-return source details, delisting treatment, or enough estimator detail to reproduce the reported strategy exactly.

## Why the result is high priority

The magnitude is economically large and directly related to the hypothesis that binary clinical failures can cause forced selling or valuation overshoot in concentrated small firms. It is also unusually vulnerable to omitted delistings, corporate-action survivorship, overlapping financing news, extreme-observation influence, and non-executable event timing.

## Replication boundary

Argus may call future work an exact replication only if it obtains the authors' event list and complete design or reconstructs a demonstrably rule-equivalent sample. The current work is an independent reconstruction. It must retain failed and disappeared securities and determine event-time size before inspecting returns.
