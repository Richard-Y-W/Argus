# 2026-08-02 — Four macro-quant discovery cycles

Richard asked Argus to broaden toward macro and quantitative research without optimizing for low latency or speed. Argus ran four literature-and-design cycles: real-time macro vintages, monetary-policy communication shocks, inflation-disagreement term structures, and Treasury supply/duration risk.

The main decision was restraint. All four questions have mature prior literatures, so none was converted directly into `HYP-025` or a backtest. The inflation-disagreement branch ranked first because the SPF offers individual forecasts, distributions, multiple horizons, and long public histories. Yet changing respondent composition and probability bins must be audited before a preregistered statistic can be defined. Real-time vintages ranked second and can supply reusable “as-of” infrastructure. Monetary-policy and Treasury-supply event studies remain attractive but depend on intraday instruments or point-in-time expectations that daily public series cannot honestly replace.

The cycles also exposed a common research principle. Final-vintage leakage, labeling a factor after seeing responses, and treating endogenous issuance as a shock are all failures to reconstruct the decision-time information set. Macro research begins with the information architecture, not the forecasting model.

## Human growth digest

- **Concept:** separate reference time, release time, and vintage time.
- **Mistake to avoid:** calling a statistical residual a structural shock because its later responses fit a story.
- **Research-design lesson:** public availability is not point-in-time availability; an event date is not an exogenous event.
- **Suggested first exercise:** explain why a balanced SPF panel and an unbalanced SPF panel answer different questions, rather than deciding that one is automatically correct.
- **Open question:** should disagreement be interpreted as heterogeneous information, heterogeneous models, or uncertainty—and what observable implication could distinguish them?
