# 2026-07-27 — Virtual economies: a domain, not yet an experiment

Richard proposed treating game currencies and other virtual assets as part of a broader digital-finance research domain rather than limiting the idea to Robux, V-Bucks, or an inflation hedge. The intuition survived initial scrutiny: digitally native consumption objects can acquire monetary and financial characteristics under different regimes of scarcity, governance, transferability, and convertibility.

The cycle narrowed the strongest first question to whether external convertibility changes price formation. It also found that generic game-economy event studies and skin return portfolios already exist. Novelty therefore cannot rest on applying familiar finance statistics to virtual prices.

Argus did not register or execute a backtest. Public documentation is rich enough to motivate research but not yet sufficient for identification. Cross-game comparisons would mix convertibility with platform identity, users, utility, market design, and survival. Display prices may also be non-executable. The next artifact should be an event-and-data inventory, with a preference for within-platform changes that create treated and untreated units.

## Human growth digest

- **Concept:** financialization is an empirical outcome, not a label assigned because an object trades digitally.
- **Mistake to avoid:** confusing documented mechanics and long price histories with identified causal variation or executable investment returns.
- **Research-design lesson:** before collecting a giant panel, search for the institutional discontinuity that gives the panel a reason to exist.
- **Open question:** which historical rule change altered convertibility for only part of a platform while leaving comparable assets or users untreated?
