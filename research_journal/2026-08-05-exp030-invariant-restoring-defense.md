# 2026-08-05 — Defense becomes measurable

Richard approved the first experiment built around financial health rather than return fitness and asked how we would know whether the immune-defense idea works. The answer became EXP-030's evaluation contract: detect an approaching leverage or liquidity breach, isolate observable contributors, restore the invariant, minimize changes to healthy modules, and compare with ordinary defenses.

Targeted defense showed the first clear surviving mechanism in the program. It beat proportional deleveraging on invariant damage in every pathology family and preserved much more healthy exposure than liquidation. But it did not pass the system claim: volatility targeting achieved lower breach area through near-total exposure reduction, and liquidation preserved more equity during severe paths.

The adversarial review caught the key attribution problem. Targeted defense acted on an early warning; proportional defense waited for a breach. We therefore cannot tell whether localization or timing produced the advantage. The next experiment is not another parameter search. It is a frozen detector-by-treatment factorial that separates those two mechanisms.

Research stage: registered synthetic mechanism benchmark and adversarial review. Attribution: collaborative. Richard approved the invariant-defense direction and asked for an understandable success criterion; Argus designed and executed the benchmark.

## Researcher takeaway

An immune defense “works” only relative to a protected quantity and a cost of intervention. EXP-030 illustrates a Pareto frontier: volatility targeting is safest because it removes nearly everything, while targeted defense accepts slightly more damage to preserve functioning. The research question is therefore not simply which policy minimizes risk, but how much viable function can be preserved for a given safety requirement.
