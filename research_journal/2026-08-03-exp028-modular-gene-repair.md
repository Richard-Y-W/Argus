# 2026-08-03 — Modular repair survives only as a conditional question

Richard asked whether the financial-genetics idea has potential after separating it from PLEX. EXP-028 moved from Argus's consumed simulator to an official frozen Kenneth French archive and compared fault-localized mutation with equal-budget unrestricted mutation, random restart, minimum variance, and equal weight.

The experiment rejected its joint claim. Localized repair beat unrestricted mutation on mean monthly certainty equivalent by 0.00368, with a paired block-bootstrap interval barely above zero, but the contrast with random restart remained uncertain and localized turnover exceeded unrestricted mutation. In the later 2025–June 2026 check, localized repair beat both stochastic searches, while minimum variance and equal weight delivered higher net return with lower volatility.

The result changes the question. “Can genetics optimize portfolios?” is too broad and too easy to rename after the fact. The useful mechanism question is whether sparse, correctly localized shocks justify preserving unaffected modules. One cross-family interaction test is warranted; mutation-scale tuning is not.

Research stage: registered historical execution and adversarial review. Attribution: human-directed. Richard chose the genetics program and asked for a potential assessment; Argus designed and executed EXP-028.

## Researcher takeaway

A plausible mechanism can produce a genuine positive sub-result and still fail as a system. Here the preserved-module idea helped relative to one comparator, but the intended turnover channel failed and simple portfolios remained formidable. The right habit is to judge the entire causal chain, not the most flattering number.
