# 2026-08-04 — The immune architecture returns, but the edge does not

Richard asked whether Argus had drifted away from his original immune-defense and adaptable-mutation idea, then authorized the direct next test. The diagnosis was partly yes: EXP-025–028 isolated mutation and inheritance, leaving surveillance, tolerance, and response escalation underdeveloped.

EXP-029 restored those elements. A frozen danger signal routed a 10-industry portfolio among no response, exact two-module repair, and systemic random regeneration while preserving incumbent weights as memory. The controller tolerated 73.3% of months, repaired locally in 3.75%, and regenerated systemically in 22.9%.

The architecture failed economically. All three certainty-equivalent superiority intervals included zero with negative point estimates. Systemic resets raised total turnover above localized and all-gene mutation despite the controller evaluating only 26.7% as many candidates. The later-vintage check again favored equal weight over the controller on return and risk.

The main conceptual correction is important: the experiments have treated portfolio fitness as organism health. That is probably the wrong vertical slice for immunity. Immune systems defend viability constraints; they do not maximize growth every period. A closer financial analogue would define a health invariant and test whether detection plus isolation restores it with less collateral intervention than standard risk controls.

Research stage: registered historical mechanism test and adversarial rejection. Attribution: collaborative. Richard originated the defense/mutation architecture and challenged drift; Argus formalized and executed the router.
