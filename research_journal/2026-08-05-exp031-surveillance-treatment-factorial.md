# 2026-08-05 — Timing, not localization, drove safety

Richard told Argus to keep going after EXP-030 found targeted defense promising but confounded. EXP-031 crossed early versus breach detection with proportional versus targeted treatment using identical restoration targets and unchanged pathology paths.

The decomposition rejected targeted-treatment superiority. At the same early warning, targeting preserved 7.27 healthy-exposure units but had a slightly higher, uncertain violation area. At breach timing it preserved 6.42 units with similarly indistinguishable safety. Only one of four non-benign families favored targeting on violation area.

Early warning was decisive under both treatments, lowering violation area by roughly 0.043–0.046. The program can now say that buffer-zone surveillance works internally and that localization reduces collateral action, but not that localization is safer.

This is progress because it removes a flattering causal story. The remaining idea is a constrained-control problem: minimize intervention subject to a preregistered safety-noninferiority bound. The current simulator is consumed; external structure is required next.

Research stage: registered mechanism decomposition and adversarial rejection. Attribution: human-directed; Richard authorized continued testing, Argus designed the factorial.
