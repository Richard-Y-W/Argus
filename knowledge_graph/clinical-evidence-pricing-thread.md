# Clinical-evidence pricing thread

## Collaborator and capacity branch

Richard's proposed early-warning branch links a clinical asset to outside organizations and their publicly disclosed commitments. The retained mechanism is costly, asset-specific external commitment; generic supply procurement is a negative-control candidate because it can support multiple assets. Insurance is currently too stale and nonspecific.

This branch connects to pre-readout scientific probability but has a distinct causal ambiguity: observed commitment can reflect technical belief, option value, government risk sharing, or demand. The first test is outcome prediction without returns. Collaborator-security spillovers remain a separate event-exposure question.

## Alpha-candidate branches after the broad-proxy rejection

The current collaborator-field probe rejects partner presence as a useful construct. Three sharper paths remain connected to the core pricing question:

```text
before readout:  commitment surprise ----> clinical outcome information ----> gated return test
at readout:      evidence quality --------> interpretation difficulty ------> post-event drift
after failure:   forced selling ----------> valuation overshoot ------------> long-horizon reversal
```

Scientific-quality drift is the leading original return question. Small-biotech failure reversal is the leading replication because a recent study reports an unusually large effect. Commitment surprise remains the leading pre-readout branch. Competitor read-through, options disagreement, financing, short interest, and operational timing remain queued and count toward the disclosed search family.

The user-proposed short-after-positive-spike branch was tested as a frozen sandbox rule and rejected in the Hwang large-firm seed. Positive events with an initial rise continued by 1.15% abnormal on average from +1 to +20, so the corresponding short lost before costs. This does not close a future small-biotech interaction, but the consumed events may not be reused to select its spike threshold or exit.

The small-biotech failure branch now has an eight-event SEC-supported, return-blind candidate ledger. A free-price audit covered only PRTA and historical AXON through the required estimation and +100 windows; six acquired, merged, or disappeared securities were absent. This makes a delisting-complete permanent-identifier return source and event-time market capitalization blocking graph nodes. No return was inspected, and the work remains an independent reconstruction because the 2025 paper's 92-event table is unavailable.

The permanent-identifier node is now executable but not yet populated. A tested CRSP CIZ gate discovers the WRDS schema, links historical names/tickers by effective date to PERMNO, and distinguishes a documented delisting-inclusive terminal row from unexplained truncation. The installed WRDS client has no configured username, so this cycle stopped before the identifier query. Event-time size, second coding, concurrent news, and borrow remain independent blockers even after CRSP access succeeds.

On 2026-08-21 the branch entered an active, reversible data-access pause after the researcher's Undergraduate/Master's WRDS request was not approved. The resume node is the institutional representative's response: CRSP access leads to the frozen identifier-only query; another vendor leads to an eight-event semantic audit; no institutional route leads to one paid trial at a time. The pause explicitly forbids using only the two Yahoo survivors.

## Current boundary — 2026-08-21

The literature establishes material sponsor-price reactions to clinical outcomes, especially for early biotechnology firms. Argus has no evidence yet that public medical information predicts those outcomes or that prices incorporate disclosed results slowly. Current ClinicalTrials.gov records are outcome-contaminated for historical decisions; exact record histories and announcement timestamps are therefore first-order inputs, not optional metadata.

No hypothesis or experiment is registered. The next edge in the graph is a return-blind, 100–200 event gold-ledger audit.
