# HYP-033 - US equal-factor decay is not dominated by one dependence cluster

*Registered 2026-09-05 before computing EXP-033 outputs. AI-led.*

## Motivation and prediction

EXP-020's equal-factor mean can be statistically stable under single-factor deletion while still being economically concentrated if many related factors in one dependence cluster contribute most of the signed estimate. If the factor-level result represents broad evidence rather than one large family, no single cluster should dominate the total signed post-publication-minus-in-sample contrast.

P1: no cluster contributes more than 40% of the signed negative total. P2: the two largest absolute cluster contributions account for at most 60% of absolute signal mass. P3: omitting any one cluster leaves the equal-factor mean at or below -0.10 percentage points per month. Survival requires all three. A single cluster contributing at least 70% of the signed negative total is a falsifier.

## Alternatives and limits

Failure would tighten the prior conclusion from "US decay is broad across factors" to "US decay is broad only before accounting for dependence-cluster concentration." The audit does not identify publication arbitrage, crowding, costs, capacity, or causal decay.

## Data, debate, and value

Inputs are the same archived EXP-020 factor contrasts and EXP-022 cluster assignments used by EXP-032. The Optimist expects no family to dominate once all factor signs are counted; the Skeptic expects the largest 59-factor family to carry most of the negative total; the Statistician requires a contribution ledger rather than only a mean; the Economist and Portfolio Manager reject practical relevance without direct implementation quantities.
