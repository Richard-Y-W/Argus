# Danger routing and financial immunity

Matzinger's danger model shifted immune initiation away from a simple self/non-self distinction toward signals associated with damage and tissue distress. Artificial immune systems later translated immune ideas into anomaly detection, fault tolerance, and optimization, including dendritic-cell algorithms that combine multiple contextual signals. Financial applications already use immune language for anomaly and manipulation detection, so terminology alone is not novel.

The narrower EXP-029 question is decision-theoretic: can damage magnitude and localization choose the least destructive intervention? The mapping is tolerance under ordinary variation, local repair under concentrated damage, and systemic regeneration under diffuse damage. This differs from pure anomaly detection because success requires better downstream control than each fixed response.

The analogy remains incomplete. Market returns do not expose tissue damage, the portfolio is not an organism, an industry is not a cell lineage, and portfolio loss is not infection. A danger signal can therefore be statistically unusual without implying that mutation—or any response—is valuable.
