# Adversarial review: biotech alpha candidate map

**Verdict:** INCONCLUSIVE  
**Claim reviewed:** at least one of eight public clinical-event mechanisms may contain tradable predictability  
**Promotion:** candidate designs may proceed; no alpha claim may be promoted

## Strongest evidence

Prior studies document large immediate reactions, unexplained cross-event variation, pre-announcement winner/loser divergence, and possible long-horizon asymmetry. One 2025 study reports an unusually large failure-reversal strategy. These findings justify replication and mechanism tests, but none validates the proposed Argus signals.

## Blocking and material risks

- **Blocking:** no reproducible, survivorship-safe clinical-event and return panel is currently local.
- **Blocking:** announcement times, delisting returns, halted sessions, financing, and concurrent news are not yet coded.
- **Material:** eight hypotheses create a multiple-testing family; only two may advance sequentially.
- **Material:** long-horizon biotech returns combine trial information with dilution, bankruptcy, acquisitions, approvals, and market regimes.
- **Material:** pre-event price separation may be private-information leakage and cannot establish public alpha.
- **Improvement:** the original commitment idea now measures surprise and counterparty downside rather than a registry label.

## Minimum next verification

Reconstruct the published 92-event failure sample or a rule-equivalent universe before measuring returns. Freeze eligibility, event timestamp, failure definition, size split, +2 to +100 window, delisting treatment, sector benchmark, weighting, and transaction-cost assumptions. Replicate the published aggregate result first; only then test cash, pipeline diversification, and exposure refinements on a development period with a later untouched confirmation period.
