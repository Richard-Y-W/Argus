# 2026-08-02 — EXP-025 financial-genome pilot

## Stage and attribution

Registered synthetic execution; **Collaborative**. Richard originated the financial-DNA, stable-genome, and mutation direction. Argus selected the first vertical slice, registered it, implemented it, and audited the result.

## Result

**NOT SUPPORTED under the joint decision rule.** Local mutation improved confirmation regret and passed the matched-risk and expected-shortfall conditions, but the recovery-time interval crossed zero. The experiment is archived as a failure with a potentially useful sub-result.

## What changed our understanding

The first test suggests that inherited neighborhoods can make search more efficient when adjacent genomes share useful structure. It simultaneously revealed the biological analogue of inbreeding: local selection collapsed diversity. The next useful mechanism is not “more mutation,” but a controlled balance between inherited local exploitation and globally novel lineages.

## Limitations

The market was synthetic, the confirmation sample was reduced to 30 paths before execution, the genome language was imposed by the simulator, and scoring began after an adaptation year. The run does not establish immediate recovery, market alpha, or literal financial genetics.

## Researcher digest

The important distinction is now operational: a **genotype** is the inherited rule specification, while a **phenotype** is the portfolio behavior it expresses. Evolution reduced genotypic diversity, but future work must also measure phenotypic exposure diversity because different code can still create the same trade.
